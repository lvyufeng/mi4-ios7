WHAT IT NAMES
  The launchctl[2] abort is a LOAD (dfsr 7) at address 0x10 (null page + 0x10). It is not a
  write, not a stack-top overrun, and not the truncated-dfar artifact: the raw DFAR is 0x10 and
  the DFSR says translation-fault-on-read. slot10 is NOT the carrier (it holds a real pointer);
  the carrier register is one not published raw — see NEXT.

OPEN (owes the next probe)
  * Which register/word held 0x10. 1033 published r0-r3, r7 and r4−sp; the 1032 panic frame's
    full register state (r4=0x2bdaa084 r5=0xffffffff r6=0x2bdaa088 r8=0x2da2e575, r12=0x4000)
    is in the same capture beside these rows — a 1034 can name the carrier by re-reading that
    frame and walking it, without a new probe, IF the pc's instruction can be decoded.
  * PC/SLIDE UNRESOLVED. Every card copy available on this host (the live card, /tmp/card.before,
    /tmp/gcache/card.bin, /mnt/data/ios7-payload/v2/ios7/rootfs.hfs — all the same HFSX volume)
    decodes pc−0x577000 = 0x37C26474 to f248 48f2 a… (sub.w r8, r8, #0x2ae), not to a load of
    far. pc−0x2C000000 (slide 0) is mid-stream garbage. So either the slide is not 0x577000, or
    the shared cache the device served differs from the payload's rootfs. The 1033 rows do not
    decide this; do not pick a fix that assumes it.
